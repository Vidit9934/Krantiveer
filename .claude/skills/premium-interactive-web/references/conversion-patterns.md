# Conversion patterns (acquisition sites)

Learned from teardowns of 15 music-industry sites (TuneCore, CD Baby, Amuse, UnitedMasters, Symphonic, Ditto, SubmitHub, Playlist Push, Feature.fm, Groover, ONErpm, LANDR, Create Music Group, Chartmetric, Saregama). A premium look gets a visitor to stay. These patterns get an artist to enroll. Use both.

**Minimal is not empty.** Keep the display layer sparse (huge type, few words). Add a conversion layer on top. A beautiful page that does not ask, prove and answer will not convert.

## Rules

1. **Login and Sign up live in the nav, top right, on every page, visible without scrolling.** Sign up is the only filled button in the bar. Log in is quiet. If there is no account system, Sign up is an enrollment form and Log in is an honest "opens soon" panel, never a fake login.
2. **The nav is solid and complete.** Opaque fill, never transparent, never hides on scroll, content starts below it so nothing shows behind. Every top-level destination is reachable from the bar (grouped mega-menus where there are many). Menus sit under the bar, not over it.
3. **One outcome headline, one verb CTA, repeated.** The CTA names the action ("Join KMF", "Plan my release") and returns at the end of every scene.
4. **Enrollment is tiny and visible early.** An inline field in the hero (paste a link, start) that opens a short form. Ask only what is needed: name, WhatsApp, a link, language, goal. Say how long it takes.
5. **Sell the dream, then the mechanism.** Open with the aspiration (a stage, a playlist, a name people sing back), then show the path, then the proof.
6. **Show the path as a real sequence.** Three or four steps. This is the legitimate home for numbers.
7. **Proof is mandatory, and honest.** Artist roster with listener figures, platform marks, press, results, quotes. Drive all of it from one data file with a `confirmed` flag. Anything unconfirmed carries a visible Placeholder tag. Never present invented content as fact; never invent a real person, company or statistic.
8. **Answer doubt before it forms.** A trust scene with the real policies and an FAQ for the obvious objections (is it bots, what does it cost, what do I need, how fast).
9. **Anchor the price and let people play.** An estimator that recommends a plan beats a three-card pricing row. Label estimates as estimates.
10. **Local specifics beat generic claims.** Name the languages and platforms the audience actually uses.

## Scene set (order by emotion, not by the stock waterfall)

hero promise with inline enroll, dream, path, proof wall, results, gallery, doors, trust, estimator, voices, FAQ, close.

## Relationship to avoid-ai-design

- Section waterfall (L9): avoided by emotion-first order and by replacing logo strips and feature cards with interactive pieces.
- Generic stat strip (L4): only with real or clearly tagged data, at uneven sizes.
- Three-tier pricing (L7) and equal cards (L2): replaced by an estimator and uneven layouts.
- Generic CTA labels (CP6) and arrow glyphs (CP3): use named actions and icon components.
