# Scene patterns

Each scene is one idea, one focal object, one motion. Word counts are budgets, not targets.

| Scene | Idea | Focal object | Motion | Words |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Preloader | Arrival | A drawing ring and wordmark | Ring draws, mark resolves, curtain lifts. Once per session. No count-up. | 1 to 3 |
| Hero (pinned) | Promise | The signature object in a light cone | Headline lines mask-in; object tilts to the pointer; on scroll the object scales and drifts while the headline dissolves | 8 to 12 |
| Statement | Belief | One huge sentence | Words go from 20% to 100% opacity as you scroll (scrub) | 5 to 12 |
| Gallery (pinned horizontal) | Proof | Large poster panels | Vertical scroll maps to sideways travel; panels tilt on hover; cursor says Drag | titles only |
| Doors | Choice | Giant words | Hover dims siblings, reveals one line and a preview; cursor says Open | 5 words + 5 lines |
| Selector | Decision | A big number | Hover or select swaps the number and three single-word benefits | under 20 |
| Close | Action | A large round button | Button is magnetic; headline reveals by line; footer is a giant wordmark | 3 to 6 |

## Rules for any scene

- Say it in 12 words or cut it. Put the rest on an inner page or behind a `details`.
- The scene's object is on screen when its words appear, and words never cover it.
- Pin only when scroll genuinely maps to progress (hero, horizontal gallery). Do not pin for decoration.
- Give each scene a different background tone so the scroll feels like moving through rooms.
- Keep a visually hidden summary and real headings for assistive tech and SEO.

## Inner pages

A giant light title, one line of at most 14 words, then ruled content. Hard copy trim: explanatory notices become `details` disclosures. Use line-mask entrances and hover reveals, not boxes.

## Product screens

Dashboards, tables and forms are exempt from the word budget but share the type, colours and cursor. Denser spacing, same shell, no ornament.
